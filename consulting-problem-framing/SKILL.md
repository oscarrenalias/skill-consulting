# Problem Framing

## Purpose
This skill turns rough inputs into a consulting-grade framing of the problem before analysis or delivery begins.

Use it to reduce ambiguity, surface hidden assumptions, and create a stable basis for discovery, proposal work, roadmap planning, or architecture assessment.

## When to use
Use this skill when:
- the request is still vague or broad
- the sponsor's desired outcome is not yet precise
- there are likely hidden constraints or competing goals
- a workshop, proposal, or assessment needs a disciplined starting point
- multiple stakeholders may define the problem differently

Do not use this skill when:
- the scope and success criteria are already documented and agreed
- the task is purely operational and does not require framing
- the request is already a mature, decision-ready brief

## Workflow context

**Upstream:** typically follows an initial client conversation, a sponsor request, or early meeting notes. No prior skill output is required — raw inputs are sufficient.

**Downstream:** the output of this skill is a natural input to an architecture or operating model review, a discovery workshop design, or a proposal. If a skill for that type of review is available, pass the problem statement and scope sections as context when invoking it.

**Delivery:** the output format produces a structured document by default. If a presentation skill is available, each output section maps to a slide: problem statement and why this matters as an opening framing slide, desired outcomes and scope as a scoping slide, assumptions and unknowns as a risk/open items slide, and recommended next step as a closing action slide.

## Inputs
Gather or infer the following where possible:
- request or initial prompt
- client context or business unit
- decision owner or sponsor
- timeline or urgency
- current pain points
- target outcomes
- explicit constraints
- implicit risks or assumptions
- known stakeholders
- available evidence or data

Where information is missing, state it plainly as unknown.

## Workflow
Follow this sequence.

### 1. Restate the ask
Summarize the request in plain business language.

### 2. Identify the real problem
Distinguish between:
- the stated request
- the likely underlying business problem
- the decision that actually needs to be made

### 3. Capture business context
Clarify:
- why this matters now
- what is driving the request
- what happens if nothing is done
- which broader initiative or operating context this belongs to

### 4. Define desired outcomes
Turn vague goals into concrete outcomes.
Prefer outcomes such as:
- reduced cost
- faster delivery
- lower operational risk
- better customer experience
- improved governance
- platform simplification
- clearer ownership

### 5. Surface constraints
List constraints across:
- time
- budget
- people and capability
- technology
- policy or compliance
- vendor commitments
- organizational politics

### 6. Set scope boundaries
State:
- what is in scope
- what is out of scope
- what is adjacent but relevant

### 7. Expose assumptions
List assumptions that are currently being made. Separate:
- confirmed facts
- working assumptions
- open questions

### 8. Define success criteria
Express success in observable terms. Include measures where possible.

### 9. Identify stakeholders
Name or classify the key parties involved:
- sponsor
- decision maker
- impacted teams
- delivery owners
- approvers or control functions

### 10. Recommend next step
Drawing on the unknowns, constraints, and success criteria identified above, recommend the most sensible immediate next step. This should follow logically from the framing rather than defaulting to a standard suggestion. Common options include:
- discovery workshop
- current-state assessment
- executive alignment session
- architecture review
- business case
- decision paper

## Output format
Always produce the following sections.

### Problem statement
A concise paragraph describing the business problem.

### Why this matters now
A concise paragraph on timing and urgency.

### Desired outcomes
A short bullet list.

### Scope
A short bullet list covering in-scope and out-of-scope.

### Constraints
A short bullet list.

### Stakeholders
A short bullet list.

### Assumptions and unknowns
Separate confirmed facts from open questions.

### Success criteria
A short bullet list with measurable or observable outcomes.

### Recommended next step
One paragraph with a concrete recommendation.

## Quality bar
A good output should:
- distinguish symptoms from root problem
- avoid solutioning too early
- be precise without sounding academic
- make uncertainty visible
- leave a sponsor with a clearer decision frame than they started with

## Failure modes to avoid
Avoid:
- repeating the client's wording without interpretation
- jumping directly into solution design
- hiding missing information
- framing everything as a technology problem when the issue is organizational or commercial
- making scope too broad to be actionable

## Style
Write in a sober consulting style.
Be concise, specific, and commercially aware.
Prefer direct statements over inflated language.
